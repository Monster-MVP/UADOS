# Literature, novelty and publication

## Literature record

Use `TEMPLATES/literature.md` to record the research question, search sources and query, dates, inclusion criteria and coverage limits. Preserve verified identifiers and links for relevant work, with claims, methods, datasets, limitations and the relationship to this project.

Do not invent references, metrics or missing details. Distinguish reading the full text from relying on an abstract or secondary description. If a source is unavailable, say so and avoid attributing unsupported conclusions to it. Respect source licenses and quotation limits.

## Novelty assessment

Compare the proposed contribution with the closest relevant work, including strong baselines and alternative explanations. State what is new, what is reused and where the evidence is incomplete. Literature coverage is bounded by the search method and date; “no matching result found” is not proof that no prior work exists.

Record changes in the research direction as reasoned decisions. Preserve rejected hypotheses and unsuccessful attempts when they influence interpretation or prevent repeated work.

## Claim-to-evidence mapping

Before submission, map every material claim to experiment IDs, raw outputs, analysis procedures and relevant sources. Verify that figures and tables reproduce from those artifacts, use consistent units and state uncertainty. Keep failed and negative findings available; do not selectively report only favorable runs.

For hybrid projects, record the code revision that produced results and distinguish a prototype demonstration from a validated general capability. Explain limitations, dataset coverage, threats to validity and replication conditions.

## Submission review

Have an independent reviewer challenge novelty, experimental design, baseline fairness, leakage, statistical interpretation and reproducibility. Resolve unsupported claims or mark them as hypotheses. Check the intended venue's current formatting, artifact, authorship and ethics requirements separately; UADOS does not encode changing venue rules.

Publication and external data sharing require the project's authorized owner's decision. The local record supports preparation and review; it does not publish a manuscript or provision a repository automatically.
